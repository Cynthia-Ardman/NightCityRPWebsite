CORE RULES
 
DEPTH: High (include scene subheaders + “Critical continuity” callouts + key quotes) FOCUS: Continuity > emotional turning points > action beats
 
 
1. IC vs OOC
 

 
If it’s clearly meta (rules, scheduling, tech issues, jokes about the players, “I’m not in character yet,” etc.) label it OOC.
 
Otherwise assume it’s IC.
 
If OOC directly changes continuity (retcon, clarification, “this is session X,” “we’re in location Y,” “this person is actually Z”), capture it as a Continuity Note.
 
 
1. Don’t invent
 

 
Never fabricate events, motives, injuries, or outcomes.
 
If attribution is unclear, write “unclear” and describe the ambiguity instead of guessing.
 
If the transcript conflicts with prior canon, flag it as a Continuity Risk (don’t resolve it).
 
 
1. Timecodes + quotes
 

 
If the transcript includes timestamps/timecodes, cite them in parentheses like (00:14:10–00:15:02).
 
If no timecodes exist, omit them entirely (don’t fake them).
 
Include short, high-signal quotes for key lines only (1–2 sentences max), especially threats, vows, reveals, lore drops, or emotional turning points.
 
 
1. Structure like a “scene cut”
 

 
Break the recap into scenes by LOCATION (or major shift in activity/vibe).
 
Each scene should have: what happened, why it matters, and the cleanest continuity facts.

Continuity: decisions, threats, messages sent, injuries, faction moves, betrayals, promises, reveals, “who knows what,” and anything that will matter later.
 
Emotional turning points: moments where a character’s state changes (spiral, confession, rupture, commitment, humiliation, bonding).
